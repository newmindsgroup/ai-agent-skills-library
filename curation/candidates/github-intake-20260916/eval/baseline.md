## 1. Assumption critique

Applied: business-model-designer, installed curated source, Version-Timestamp 2026-09-15 18:53:24 AST. Its narrow-scope and evidence/calculation rules apply directly.

### Output

Interest does not establish purchase demand. Six positive interviews and two nonbinding letters provide no paid conversion evidence. The sample is small, and its representativeness is unknown. Delivery capacity of 20 projects per month does not mean 20 customers will buy immediately.

At the stated figures, contribution is USD 400 per project. Monthly break-even is 13 projects: USD 5,000 / USD 400, rounded up. At 20 projects, the stated operating surplus is USD 3,000 per month, before omitted costs, taxes and any founder compensation not already included. Price acceptance, variable costs and capacity remain unvalidated assumptions.

Test willingness to pay first: offer a clearly scoped paid pilot at USD 1,000 to qualified prospects, including the two letter signers. Record offers, actual purchases, objections and time to close. Choose the test period and evidence threshold before starting. Then measure delivery effort and actual variable costs. Do not infer reliable monthly demand from one pilot.

### Outcome and limits

Within 180 words. Distinguishes interest, payment, capacity and demand; computes contribution, break-even and stated surplus correctly; proposes first test without pretending a threshold is established. Existing skill already provides substantial coverage for this request.

## Overall interpretation

All three synthetic outputs satisfy the visible numerical and safety requirements. Improvements from candidate skills should therefore be judged on repeatability, method specificity, difficult edge cases and routing fit, not merely whether they can pass these straightforward cases. A single successful baseline cannot establish comparative superiority or malicious-code absence in any candidate.
