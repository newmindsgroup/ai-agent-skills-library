# Follow-up drafts

Draft a sequence only when requested. Each message should offer a relevant new detail, answer a likely question, or provide a useful resource supported by the available evidence. Do not invent a case study or statistic to create a new angle.

Choose the proposed number and spacing of messages for the context. Label an untested cadence as a proposal; do not claim a universally optimal day, time, or sequence length. Respect existing contact preferences and any applicable operational rules.

A final outreach message should mean the outreach stops. Do not resume after silence. A recipient's explicit request for later contact can establish a new agreed follow-up; an opt-out remains controlling. Never use guilt, fake urgency, or invented scarcity to induce a reply.

A simple closing draft: "I will leave it here. If a workflow review becomes useful, you are welcome to reply." Use only if it fits the real sequence and user voice.

Drafting does not schedule or send messages. For actual execution, verify the authorized recipients, content, channel, timing and suppression handling. For a one-email request, do not expand into a sequence or research task.
