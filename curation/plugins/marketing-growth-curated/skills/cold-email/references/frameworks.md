# Draft structures

Choose a structure that fits the supplied facts. These patterns are optional, not evidence that one always performs better.

## Offer and question
Use when little recipient evidence is available. State the actual offer and ask one relevant, low-friction question. Example: "I offer a workflow review. Would a short outline be useful?" Do not invent a pain point to fill the structure.

## Observation, implication, question
Use a verified observation, label any inference as an inference, then ask whether the issue is relevant. Do not assume team growth, spending, targets, or internal problems from a job title.

## Problem, approach, next step
Use a problem the recipient has actually expressed or pose it conditionally. Explain the real approach without exaggerating consequences. End with the requested next step.

## Evidence and relevance
Use a customer example only when its identity, result, permission and applicability are established. State the measured result with context. If proof is unavailable, omit it. Never substitute a famous company or fictional success story.

## Constraints
Preserve the user's requested length and voice. Do not force a compliment, urgency, story, or case study. A framework should help organize real information, not create facts. No template guarantees replies or justifies sending an unsolicited campaign.
