# Accurate subject lines

Use a brief subject that accurately describes the message. A reader should not have to mistake the sender for a colleague or previous contact to open it.

Examples, only when they match the actual message: "workflow review", "weekly reporting", "a question about onboarding". Use a project or initiative only when supplied or verified. "Thank you" requires something real to thank the recipient for; "an update" requires an actual update with clear context.

Do not imply employment, a prior exchange, an existing account, urgency, or an obligation that does not exist. Do not add fake Re: or Fwd: prefixes. Without recipient-specific evidence, use a neutral topic rather than invented personalization.

Fit the subject to the user's voice and audience. Check readability on the intended device when relevant. No word count, capitalization style, or punctuation choice guarantees a higher response rate.

For an authorized experiment, vary one meaningful element, use comparable recipients, and define the outcome and denominator. Treat open tracking as an imperfect signal. Prefer relevant replies and outcomes over opening alone. For a draft-only request, supply the subject and message without expanding into an experiment.
