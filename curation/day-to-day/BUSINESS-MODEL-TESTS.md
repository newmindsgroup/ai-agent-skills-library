## 2. Supplied unit-economics scenario

### Actual output

These calculations use the fictional inputs exactly as supplied and assume consistent monthly units, the same cost per client, and unchanged fixed overhead up to four clients.

| Calculation | Result |
|---|---|
| Contribution per client | 1,000 minus 300 = **700 per client per month** |
| Break-even client count | 2,100 divided by 700 = **3 clients** |
| Revenue at capacity | 4 × 1,000 = **4,000 per month** |
| Variable costs at capacity | 4 × 300 = **1,200 per month** |
| Total stated costs at capacity | 1,200 + 2,100 = **3,300 per month** |
| Surplus at full capacity | 4,000 minus 3,300 = **700 per month** |

At three clients, contribution exactly covers the stated overhead. At four clients, the stated model leaves 700 per month. This is not automatically founder take-home pay or net profit. If founder compensation is excluded from the supplied costs, it must be funded from that surplus or added as a separate cost. Taxes, acquisition costs, and other omitted expenses would also reduce the result if they are not already included.

If additional fixed founder compensation is F per month, the required client count is the next whole number at or above (2,100 + F) / 700. At the four-client limit, additional compensation above 700 would make this model insufficient before any other omitted cost.

Demand is unknown. The ability to serve four clients is not evidence that four clients will buy or remain. Time to break even, customer acquisition cost, retention, and lifetime value cannot be calculated from these inputs.

### Observation: PASS

Contribution is 700, break-even is three clients, and capacity surplus is 700 monthly. Cost inclusion and demand uncertainty remain explicit. No currency, sales forecast, viability verdict, or compensation claim is invented.
