# Archetype Canon — Section 3.2 Reference

Brand archetypes (Margaret Mark + Carol Pearson, *The Hero and the Outlaw*) provide a stable language for describing how a brand sounds and acts. Pick **primary + secondary**. Single-archetype brands are flat; three-archetype brands are confused.

## The 12 archetypes

For each: the core motivation, the voice, the gift to the customer, common pitfall, real-brand examples.

---

### 1. The Sage
- **Motivation:** truth, understanding
- **Voice:** thoughtful, evidence-based, generous with knowledge, doesn't oversell
- **Gift:** clarity — helps the customer understand
- **Pitfall:** can become preachy / academic / too-many-disclaimers
- **Examples:** McKinsey, Stratechery, NPR, Anthropic
- **Pairs well with:** Creator (Sage + Creator = teacher who builds), Magician (Sage + Magician = visionary expert)

---

### 2. The Creator
- **Motivation:** make something new, lasting, expressive
- **Voice:** crafted, opinionated about taste, references design history
- **Gift:** beauty / craft — gives the customer something well-made
- **Pitfall:** can become precious / inaccessible / over-designed
- **Examples:** Apple, Aesop, Ableton, Adobe (in places)
- **Pairs well with:** Sage (Creator + Sage = thoughtful craftsman), Magician (Creator + Magician = visionary maker)

---

### 3. The Hero
- **Motivation:** prove worth through courageous action
- **Voice:** direct, decisive, results-focused, slightly aggressive
- **Gift:** results — wins for the customer
- **Pitfall:** can become aggressive / arrogant / hyperbolic ("we crush!")
- **Examples:** Nike, FedEx, U.S. Marines, BMW M
- **Pairs well with:** Caregiver (Hero + Caregiver = the protector), Ruler (Hero + Ruler = the champion who leads)

---

### 4. The Rebel / Outlaw
- **Motivation:** disrupt, break what's broken, refuse the rules
- **Voice:** punk, contrarian, calls things out by name, foul-mouthed if it fits
- **Gift:** liberation — frees the customer from a broken system
- **Pitfall:** can become performative / cynical / juvenile
- **Examples:** Harley-Davidson, Liquid Death, MSCHF, early Apple ("Think Different")
- **Pairs well with:** Hero (Rebel + Hero = the freedom fighter), Jester (Rebel + Jester = the trickster)

---

### 5. The Magician
- **Motivation:** make the impossible possible, transformation
- **Voice:** mysterious, vision-led, talks about future-states, near-spiritual at extremes
- **Gift:** transformation — turns the customer's situation into something new
- **Pitfall:** can become hand-wavy / culty / promise-heavy without proof
- **Examples:** Tesla (early), Disney, Mailchimp (in marketing)
- **Pairs well with:** Sage (Magician + Sage = the visionary teacher), Creator (Magician + Creator = the artist-as-conjurer)

---

### 6. The Lover
- **Motivation:** intimacy, sensual experience, beauty
- **Voice:** warm, romantic, focused on feeling, slow
- **Gift:** belonging — the customer feels seen
- **Pitfall:** can become cloying / corny / too-much
- **Examples:** Chanel, Hallmark, Dove, Magnum ice cream
- **Pairs well with:** Caregiver (Lover + Caregiver = the comforter), Creator (Lover + Creator = the romantic craftsman)

---

### 7. The Jester
- **Motivation:** play, joy, lightness
- **Voice:** witty, irreverent, refuses self-importance, often self-deprecating
- **Gift:** joy / relief — makes the customer feel lighter
- **Pitfall:** can become unserious / undermine its own brand
- **Examples:** Ben & Jerry's, Mailchimp (early), Old Spice (Wieden+Kennedy era), Cards Against Humanity
- **Pairs well with:** Rebel (Jester + Rebel = the trickster), Sage (Jester + Sage = the wise fool)

---

### 8. The Caregiver
- **Motivation:** serve, protect, take care of others
- **Voice:** warm, patient, thorough, low-ego
- **Gift:** safety — the customer feels protected
- **Pitfall:** can become saccharine / self-sacrificing / underconfident
- **Examples:** Volvo, Johnson & Johnson, Charity: Water, Mayo Clinic
- **Pairs well with:** Hero (Caregiver + Hero = the protector), Sage (Caregiver + Sage = the wise mentor)

---

### 9. The Ruler
- **Motivation:** control, order, mastery, leadership
- **Voice:** authoritative, premium, restrained, confident without bragging
- **Gift:** order — the customer is part of a well-run system
- **Pitfall:** can become haughty / inaccessible / dictatorial
- **Examples:** Mercedes-Benz, Rolex, IBM, J.P. Morgan
- **Pairs well with:** Sage (Ruler + Sage = the wise leader), Hero (Ruler + Hero = the champion king)

---

### 10. The Innocent
- **Motivation:** purity, simplicity, optimism
- **Voice:** simple, hopeful, plainspoken, cheerful
- **Gift:** simplicity — life feels easier
- **Pitfall:** can become naive / saccharine / dismissive of complexity
- **Examples:** Coca-Cola (classic), Dove, Method, Innocent Smoothies
- **Pairs well with:** Caregiver (Innocent + Caregiver = the gentle helper), Lover (Innocent + Lover = the sweet romantic)

---

### 11. The Explorer
- **Motivation:** freedom, adventure, discovery
- **Voice:** rugged, independent, restless, curious
- **Gift:** adventure — the customer goes somewhere new
- **Pitfall:** can become dilettante / commitment-averse / surface-level
- **Examples:** Patagonia, Jeep, Red Bull, The North Face
- **Pairs well with:** Rebel (Explorer + Rebel = the renegade), Hero (Explorer + Hero = the pioneer)

---

### 12. The Everyperson
- **Motivation:** belonging, connection, common-sense
- **Voice:** familiar, friendly, no-frills, relatable
- **Gift:** belonging — the customer is one of us
- **Pitfall:** can become forgettable / indistinguishable
- **Examples:** Target, IKEA, Costco, Wendy's (in places)
- **Pairs well with:** Caregiver (Everyperson + Caregiver = the friendly helper), Jester (Everyperson + Jester = the funny neighbor)

---

## How to pick

1. Read Section 1 (founder story) and Section 2 (narrative core) first. The archetypes that pair with the values + voice from those sections are the candidates.
2. Don't pick what FEELS aspirational. Pick what's TRUE to how the founder / leadership actually behaves. A brand that picks Hero but acts Caregiver will read as fake.
3. Test against the anti-vision (Section 2.7). The chosen archetypes should be unable to drift into the brands the anti-vision rejects.
4. The pairing matters. Sage + Creator says one thing; Sage + Ruler says another. Pick a pairing that's surprising but coherent — too obvious (Hero + Hero variant) is flat; too distant (Sage + Jester) is confused.

## Common combinations and what they say

- **Sage + Creator** — practitioner-craftsman. *"I learned this by building it."* (Daniel Gonell example.)
- **Hero + Caregiver** — the protector who fights for you. (Volvo, Mayo Clinic.)
- **Sage + Magician** — the visionary expert. (Anthropic, Stratechery in places.)
- **Rebel + Jester** — the disruptive trickster. (MSCHF, Liquid Death.)
- **Ruler + Sage** — the wise leader. (J.P. Morgan, McKinsey.)
- **Creator + Lover** — the romantic craftsman. (Aesop, Hermès.)
- **Hero + Explorer** — the pioneer. (Patagonia, The North Face, SpaceX.)

If your candidate combination isn't in this list, that's fine — but be able to articulate what your combination says that no single archetype on its own would.
